\caption{Results obtained by concatenating \textsc{sem} to the predictors considered in the study (\textsc{emb}, \textsc{sfl}, \textsc{emb}$^\frown$\textsc{sfl}). The bars on top of blue, green, and red bars indicate the difference in model fit that can be ascribed to \textsc{sem}; these bars are orange when the difference in model fit is positive (i.e., \textsc{sem} improves the fit to neural and behavioral data) and dark brown if negative. The asterisks indicate the statistical significance of the contrast between the \say{base} model (\textsc{emb}, \textsc{sfl}, \textsc{emb}$^\frown$\textsc{sfl}) and the corresponding model with the addition of \textsc{sem} (\textsc{emb}$^\frown$\textsc{sem}, \textsc{sfl}$^\frown$\textsc{sem}, and \textsc{emb}$^\frown$\textsc{sfl}$^\frown$\textsc{sem}). The error bars indicate the standard error of the mean of the results obtained with the addition of \textsc{sem} across the validation folds. The addition of \textsc{sem} to \textsc{sfl} is associated with a numerically small increase in predictivity in the behavioral datasets, whereas in the Pereira and Michaelov datasets the performance improves by a more substantial margin. The addition of \textsc{sem} to \textsc{emb} and \textsc{emb}$^\frown$\textsc{sfl} has a null or negligible effect across both behavioral and neural datasets. }
