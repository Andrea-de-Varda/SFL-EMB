\caption{\textbf{A}: graphical summary of the results obtained in the various experiments. The height of the bars indicates the average $r$ obtained in the 10 folds, and the error bars report the standard error of the mean. As most of the comparisons (\textsc{emb}, \textsc{sfl}, \textsc{emb}$^\frown$\textsc{sfl}) obtained significantly different results, for readability we annotate the model pairs whose performances were \textit{not} significantly different. The diagram on the right summarizes the comparison between \textsc{emb} and \textsc{sfl}. The gray lines connect the psychometric and neural datasets to their best predictor among \textsc{sfl} and \textsc{emb}; the width of the line is proportional to the difference between the results obtained with the two methodologies. The behavioral datasets are colored in green, while neural datasets are colored in blue. The figure shows that \textsc{sfl} is generally associated with higher levels of predictivity in the behavioral datasets, while \textsc{emb} achieves better results in the neural datasets. \textbf{B}: model-focused summary of the results, aggregated across behavioral and neural datasets. The $r$ values obtained with the different models in each dataset are $z$-scored. The black squares specify the mean value of the results obtained by each model, while the horizontal lines indicate the median. }
